Fiscal anticipation and inequality: the role of the monetary-fiscal mix

  • Vedanta Dhamija
    Centre for Macroeconomics
  • Francesco Fusari
    Newcastle University Business School
  • Roshni Tara
    Centre for Macroeconomics
Download PDF

Abstract

Fiscal policy is often anticipated before implementation, yet little is known about how fiscal foresight shapes inequality. We study the distributional effects of government spending shocks, distinguishing between anticipated (news) and unanticipated (surprise) components. We show that positive surprise shocks reduce U.S. consumption inequality, whereas positive news shocks increase it. A two-agent New Keynesian (TANK) model rationalises these findings and highlights the role of monetary-fiscal regimes: in a monetary-led regime, news and surprise shocks have opposite distributional effects similar to the empirical findings, while in a fiscally-led regime both reduce consumption inequality. Our results demonstrate that the distributional impact of fiscal policy depends critically on fiscal foresight and the prevailing policy regime.
